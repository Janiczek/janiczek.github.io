# Freedom to create jank

I want to quickly comment on a change in myself I was observing for the past few
months.

## The past

Previously I was self-critical towards my projects and what I put out into the
world, and demanded a certain level of quality or outward polish out of my
creations. "Oh this project needs a great README on Github before I publish it,
oh this game needs better graphics before I let anybody see it, oh the blog
needs to be redesigned."

The recent wave of LLM slop which has questionable quality but _GREAT DESIGN_
by default (although now recognizable and increasingly off-putting because of
the association); landing pages with beautiful serif headings and monospace
menus, READMEs with a ton of emojis and 20 different subheadings, _all of this
makes me crave authenticity_ instead of professionalism and frees me to show
the human inside my creations instead, however janky it might look.

Michael Lynch of ["Refactoring English"](https://refactoringenglish.com/) said it best:

[![Even a terrible MS Paint drawing is more interesting than an AI-generated image.](/assets/images/2026-08-21-freedom-to-create-jank/michael-lynch-ai-vs-mspaint.webp)](https://refactoringenglish.com/excerpts/write-blog-posts-developers-read/#show-more-pictures)

> [Even a terrible MS Paint drawing is more interesting than an AI-generated image.](https://refactoringenglish.com/excerpts/write-blog-posts-developers-read/#show-more-pictures:~:text=Even%20a%20terrible%20MS%20Paint%20drawing%20is%20more%20interesting%20than%20an%20AI%2Dgenerated%20image.) ~Michael Lynch

## The present

I'm now at the point where as I'm working on a game, I am fully prepared to just
scribble some sprites on paper or make a crude pixel art drawing _and keep it in
the final game_ instead of hiring a professional designer to make something,
well, polished. I'm suddenly free to put out something on the internet even if
it doesn't have a landing page and a Github stars tracker.

![Pixelart 1](/assets/images/2026-08-21-freedom-to-create-jank/slingshot@4x.png) ![Pixelart 2](/assets/images/2026-08-21-freedom-to-create-jank/pebbles@4x.png)

The game [Nepenthe](https://yitzilitt.itch.io/nepenthe) comes to mind as I write
this blogpost:

![Nepenthe](/assets/images/2026-08-21-freedom-to-create-jank/nepenthe.jpg)

Is it janky? Oh you have no idea how janky it is. Did I enjoy it? Yes, yes I
did.

Olivia When has written [a BlueSky thread about "Some things lost to
AI."](https://bsky.app/profile/oliviawhen.com/post/3mtkwy53gdc2n)

!["I like every placeholder drawing even if you hate it."](/assets/images/2026-08-21-freedom-to-create-jank/olivia-when-i-like-every-placeholder-drawing.png)

It resonates with me and speaks about the hunger for authenticity. _"I like
every weird handmade poster. I like the joke you spent 20 minutes crafting. I
like every placeholder drawing even if you hate it."_

I believe those are _not_ things we lost to AI. I believe AI has just awoken the
hunger for them by providing contrast: polished slop without soul. It has
certainly lowered the barrier to creation for me, and lessened my fear of "is it
good enough?"

What's important has changed. Personality and authenticity over polish. Embrace
the jank. Put your soul into something and put it out there. We'll celebrate it
because _you_ did it.
